Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about the Old and New income tax regimes in India.

Which tax regime should I choose: Old or New?

It depends on how many deductions and exemptions you claim. If you have large investments under Section 80C, HRA, or a home loan, the Old Regime may work out cheaper. If you claim few deductions, the New Regime's lower slab rates usually save more. Use the comparison above with your actual numbers to be sure. It's different for every income and deduction combination.

What is the standard deduction under each regime?

Salaried employees and pensioners get a standard deduction of ₹75,000 under the New Regime and ₹50,000 under the Old Regime for FY 2025-26. It is deducted automatically from gross income before tax is calculated, with no bills or proofs required.

What is the Section 87A rebate?

It's a rebate that can reduce your tax to zero for lower incomes. Under the New Regime, taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 attracts no tax after rebate (effectively ₹12,75,000 for salaried taxpayers, once the ₹75,000 standard deduction is applied). Under the Old Regime, the rebate applies up to ₹5,00,000 taxable income. Just above these limits, marginal relief ensures your tax never exceeds the amount your income exceeds the threshold by.

What counts as "taxable income" in this calculator?

Taxable income is your gross annual income minus the standard deduction (if you're salaried) and, under the Old Regime, any Chapter VI-A deductions you enter: Section 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B), HRA exemption, home loan interest, and others. Tax is then calculated slab-by-slab on this amount.

Do I have to stick with the same regime every year?

Salaried individuals without business income can switch between the Old and New Regime every financial year when filing their return. If you have business or professional income, switching back to the Old Regime after opting for the New Regime is restricted, so choose carefully.

What are surcharge and marginal relief?

Surcharge is an additional charge on the income tax itself, applicable once taxable income crosses ₹50 lakh, rising in steps up to ₹5 crore and beyond. Marginal relief ensures that if you're just above a surcharge threshold, the extra tax and surcharge you pay never exceeds the extra income you earned over that threshold. This calculator applies both automatically.

What is the Health & Education Cess?

A flat 4% cess is added on top of income tax plus surcharge, under both regimes, to fund health and education initiatives. It applies to every taxpayer with a tax liability. There's no exemption or threshold for it.

Does this calculator account for TDS or advance tax already paid?

No, this tool calculates your total annual tax liability only. It doesn't track tax already deducted at source (TDS) or advance tax instalments paid during the year. Compare the total liability shown here against Form 26AS/AIS to work out any balance payable or refund due.

Is this a substitute for professional tax advice?

No. This calculator is for illustrative planning purposes only, based on tax slabs notified for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27). Rates can change with subsequent Finance Acts, and individual situations can involve nuances this tool doesn't cover.
